This guide covers common tasks in the World Model. For what the graph shows and how it's laid out, see the [World Model reference](/world-model).

## Finding a service

Click **World Model** in the sidebar, then use the search bar at the top of the canvas to type a service name. The graph zooms to the matching node and highlights it.

Pan by dragging the background and zoom with the scroll wheel. The controls in the bottom-left corner include a fit-view button that frames the whole graph again.

## Focusing on a service's neighborhood

Right-click a node and select **Focus**. The graph hides everything except nodes within a few hops of that service, and a depth slider lets you widen or narrow the neighborhood.

To go back to the full graph, click the reset button that appears, or right-click and select **Reset view**.

## Tracing dependencies

Hover a node to highlight it and its edges while everything else dims. Flow dots travel along each edge in the direction of the call.

Click an edge to see its relationship type in the detail panel: calls, depends-on, reads-from, and so on.

When a service in one cluster calls a service in another, the connection appears as a bundled line between container borders. Hover the bundle to see how many relationships it carries and of what type.

## Viewing service details

Click a node to open the detail panel, then click **View full details** for the full-page view. Overview shows the service's properties along with its linked documents, PagerDuty services, and Jira issues. Connections lists its outbound calls. History shows past PR reviews from Foresight and any incidents involving the service.

## Asking questions about the topology

Right-click any node and select **Ask Cielara**. The assistant opens with a prompt already describing that node and its relationships, so you can go straight to a follow-up question.

You can also open Ask Cielara from any page and ask about the topology directly, for example "What services depend on the payments API?" or "Show me the blast radius if Redis goes down."

## Managing favorites

Right-click a node and select **Favorite**, or click the star on its card. Favorites are saved per user, so your starred services stay marked across sessions.
